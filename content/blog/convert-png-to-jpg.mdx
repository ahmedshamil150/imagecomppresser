## When converting PNG to JPG makes sense

PNG is lossless, which is why it stores screenshots, logos and icons so well — but it is the wrong format for photographs and detailed images. A 4000×3000 photo saved as PNG can weigh 8 MB; the same photo as a quality-85 JPG often lands around 400 KB.

Convert PNG to JPG when:

- The image is a **photo, scan, or textured screenshot** destined for the web
- You need to **meet an upload limit** (many forms cap files at 1–2 MB)
- The image has **no meaningful transparency** (or white background is acceptable)
- You are **emailing or embedding** the image and want fast loading

Keep PNG when the graphic has transparent areas, hard-edged logos, or text that must stay pixel-perfect.

## How much smaller does the JPG get?

Realistic savings depend on content:

- **Photograph PNG → JPG:** 85–95% smaller
- **Screenshot PNG → JPG (quality 90):** 40–70% smaller
- **Illustration PNG → JPG:** 30–60% smaller (check for ringing around sharp lines)

If a screenshot's text becomes fuzzy at quality 85, raise it to 92 — the file is still far smaller than the PNG original.

## JPG settings that actually matter

- **Chroma subsampling:** most encoders default to 4:2:0, which slightly softens colored text. Quality 90+ generally compensates enough for screen use.
- **Dimensions:** [resize before converting](/resize-image) if the PNG is larger than where it will be displayed. Dimension cuts beat quality cuts every time.
- **Re-saving:** every save re-compresses. Convert once from the original PNG, not repeatedly from the JPG.

## What about PNG to WebP instead?

If your target is a modern website, [PNG to WebP](/png-to-webp) gives you transparency *and* smaller files — usually beating JPG outright. JPG remains the answer when compatibility with older software or upload forms is the priority.

## FAQ

### Does PNG to JPG conversion lose quality?

Yes — JPG is lossy, so some data is discarded. At quality 85–90 the change is hard to spot on photos and screenshots. If you need guaranteed pixel-perfect output, keep PNG.

### What happens to transparent backgrounds?

JPG has no alpha channel, so transparent pixels are flattened onto white. If you need transparency preserved, convert to [WebP](/png-to-webp) instead.
